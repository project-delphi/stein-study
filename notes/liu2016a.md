# liu2016a — A Kernelized Stein Discrepancy for Goodness-of-fit Tests

Status: VERIFIED (arXiv:1602.03253 record and PMLR v48 page; arXiv title carries the suffix "and Model Evaluation").

## Claim
Combining Stein's identity with an RKHS gives a computable discrepancy (KSD) between samples and a target density p. It needs only p's score, so unnormalised targets work, and it gives goodness-of-fit tests for high dimension.

## Method
KSD^2 is the expectation of a Stein kernel u_p(x,x') over independent x,x' from q. u_p is built from the base kernel, the score grad log p, and kernel derivatives. It vanishes iff q = p for a suitable (cc-universal) kernel. Estimated by a U-statistic (unbiased, quadratic cost); bootstrap null. Only the target score is used. Kernel: RBF, median-heuristic bandwidth. Full, not sliced.

## Overlap with this study
Source definition of KSD. A Gaussian prior has a closed-form score; so does any matched non-Gaussian prior with a known score. No JEPA, autoencoder, nuisance, bottleneck or identifiability content.

## Open gap
KSD as a training regulariser (IMQ kernel, gradients, small batches, high dimension), sliced KSD, and comparison with SIGReg, VICReg, MMD and EMA under JEPA bottlenecks on real data with known factors.
